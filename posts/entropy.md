Entropy becomes less mysterious when you stop treating it as a synonym for “disorder” and start tracking possible microscopic arrangements.

Imagine a box divided into two halves. If a gas starts entirely on one side and the partition is removed, there are vastly more ways for the molecules to occupy the whole box than to remain on one side.

$$S = k_B \ln \Omega$$

Here, $\Omega$ is the number of compatible microscopic states.

The second law tells us that an isolated system naturally evolves toward macrostates that correspond to overwhelmingly more microscopic possibilities.
