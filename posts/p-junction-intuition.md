A p-n junction becomes easier to understand when you stop thinking of it as two labels and start thinking about carrier diffusion and the electric field that develops at the boundary.

When p-type and n-type material meet, majority carriers initially diffuse across the junction. The uncovered ionized dopants create a depletion region and an internal electric field.

Under forward bias, the externally applied voltage opposes part of the built-in barrier, making carrier injection easier. Under reverse bias, the barrier is reinforced and the depletion region widens.

This picture is more useful than memorizing which side “has more electrons” because it lets you reason about what happens when the circuit conditions change.
